Sigmoid Hacks × Hack Club

Building a High-School Developer Community Through Strategic Partnership

357 participants · High-school hackathon · Community partnership

The Opportunity

In 2021, we founded Sigmoid Hacks, a hackathon designed to bring ambitious high-school students together to build, learn, and connect with other young developers.

To strengthen the event's community impact, we partnered with Hack Club, a nonprofit dedicated to creating community and providing learning opportunities for ambitious high-school students.

The Partnership

We collaborated with Hack Club to bring its community and mission into Sigmoid Hacks, creating an experience that went beyond simply hosting a hackathon.

A key part of the partnership was featuring Hack Club founder Zach Latta as a featured speaker during the opening ceremony, giving participants direct exposure to one of the leading voices in the high-school developer community.

The Result

357 students participated in Sigmoid Hacks, creating a large-scale opportunity for high-school developers to build projects, learn from one another, and engage with the broader developer community.

The partnership also generated significant visibility for Hack Club by putting its founder and mission directly in front of hundreds of ambitious student developers at the beginning of the event.

Impact

  • 357 participants
  • High-school developer audience
  • Hack Club partnership
  • Zach Latta featured as opening ceremony speaker
  • Increased exposure for Hack Club among aspiring young developers

Why It Worked

Rather than treating the partnership as a simple sponsorship or promotional arrangement, we integrated Hack Club directly into the event experience.

By putting the organization's founder on stage at the opening ceremony, participants were introduced to Hack Club at one of the most visible moments of the event—creating a direct connection between the organization and a highly relevant audience.

SB Hacks × Unwrap

Putting an AI Company in Front of UCSB’s Builder Community

300+ student builders · University hackathon · Santa Barbara tech partnership

The Opportunity

SB Hacks (sbhacks.com) is UC Santa Barbara’s flagship student hackathon — a weekend where hundreds of undergraduate and graduate builders ship projects, meet mentors, and engage directly with sponsors inside Corwin Pavilion.

For a company like Unwrap — an AI-powered customer intelligence platform building in California — that room is not a broad awareness channel. It is a concentrated audience of high-intent engineers already choosing to spend a weekend building.

The Partnership

We worked to bring Unwrap into SB Hacks as a meaningful presence in the event, not just a logo on a slide deck. Unwrap joined as a sponsor with direct engagement: company representatives participated as judges, met teams on the floor, and connected with builders evaluating real-world AI and product problems.

That same local talent pipeline later showed up again when Unwrap hosted its own hands-on event, the Unwrapathon, where students collaborated with Unwrap engineers on NLP, insight delivery, and customer-intelligence challenges — with mentorship and hiring pathways built into the format.

The Result

Unwrap gained recurring visibility with UCSB’s developer community across SB Hacks editions — sitting alongside major sponsors while staying close to the builders who actually ship.

More importantly, the collaboration created a path from event presence to deeper engagement: judging and floor conversations at SB Hacks, then a company-hosted hackathon where students solved problems Unwrap itself cares about.

Impact

  • 300+ student builders at UCSB’s premier hackathon
  • Sponsor + judging presence for Unwrap at SB Hacks
  • Direct access to high-intent university engineering talent
  • Follow-on Unwrapathon with mentorship and hiring signal
  • Local Santa Barbara tech community continuity beyond a one-weekend logo

Why It Worked

University hackathons reward presence. Unwrap did not buy interruption — it entered a room where motivated engineers had already opted in to build, present, and talk to companies.

By pairing sponsorship at SB Hacks with a deeper, company-owned experience afterward, Unwrap turned a single weekend of visibility into an ongoing relationship with the same talent market.

Entrepreneurs First × Showerhacks

Hosting Ambitious Builders Inside a Founder Pipeline

400-person SF hackathon · Venue partnership · Founder-talent audience

The Opportunity

Entrepreneurs First (EF) turns exceptional individuals into funded founders — backing people first, then giving them a bridge into Silicon Valley. That model depends on proximity to ambitious builders, not just people already pitching polished startups.

Showerhacks (showerhacks.org) is a 12-hour San Francisco hackathon built for absurdly creative ideas: hundreds of engineers, designers, and early founders shipping wild projects in a single day, with a first-place prize that includes — literally — a shower.

The Partnership

We partnered with Entrepreneurs First to host Showerhacks at EF’s San Francisco space on Folsom Street, putting the organization at the center of the event experience rather than at the edge of a sponsor reel.

EF became the physical home for the day: check-in, hacking, judging, and the closing ritual all happened inside a venue already associated with founding exceptional companies. Participants did not just see EF’s name — they spent twelve hours building inside its house.

The Result

Showerhacks gained a premium San Francisco venue and a natural narrative fit with founder culture. Entrepreneurs First gained day-long immersion with a curated crowd of builders, creators, and potential founders — the exact profile EF exists to find and back.

Instead of competing for attention in a feed, EF sat at the intersection of creativity, shipping energy, and post-event conversation — including an afterparty for top teams where relationships continue after demos end.

Impact

  • ~400-person San Francisco hackathon audience
  • Venue partnership at Entrepreneurs First (501 Folsom St)
  • Founder-adjacent builders in the room for a full day
  • Brand immersion through hosting, not just sponsorship logos
  • Afterparty continuity with top teams after the build ends

Why It Worked

The highest-leverage partnership for a talent investor is not a banner — it is proximity. Hosting Showerhacks put Entrepreneurs First inside the workflow of the day: the place people arrived, built, presented, and remembered.

That is the point of these collaborations. When the venue, the audience, and the mission already overlap, the brand does not need to interrupt anyone. It is already where serious people chose to be.
