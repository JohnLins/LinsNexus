Why attention on feeds is the wrong bet for serious audiences

Marketing on platforms like Meta is built on a selection bias most brands never measure. The people you most want to reach — professionals, researchers, operators, and decision-makers — are not spending their evenings doomscrolling Instagram. They are deep in work, in communities, and in conversations that matter. Feed advertising optimizes for who is available to be interrupted, not for who is ready to engage with intent.

That bias gets worse as platforms monetize attention. Pro subscriptions across YouTube, Spotify, X, and others increasingly disable ads for paying users. The result is structural: your message is disproportionately shown to people who have not paid to remove noise — an audience skewed toward lower willingness to pay. You are bidding for reach among the users least likely to convert into high-value relationships.

Meanwhile, people have adapted. Years of low-trust creative, lookalike targeting, and endless sponsored posts trained audiences to phase out online noise. When everything claims authority, trust becomes scarce. The question is no longer “Did they see the ad?” It is “Would they believe us if they did?”

Events and lived experiences reverse that dynamic. A sponsorship at a conference, salon, or hands-on activation is not another tile in a scroll — it is a memory. Presence in a room signals commitment. Higher commitment from the sponsor invites higher intentionality from the audience: questions get asked, demos get tried, conversations continue after the session ends.

That is how brands secure top talent, earn time with market leaders, meet researchers where they already gather, and connect with motivated students before the inbox war begins. Not by winning the feed — by showing up where serious people already chose to be.
